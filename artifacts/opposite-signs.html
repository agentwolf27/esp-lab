Identity, Not Context

Ask a frozen audio model what situation an animal was in and it answers surprisingly well — until you stop it recognising which animal, or which lab. Across cats, pigs and wild bats, identity and recording site are decodable at 0.63–0.82 and inflate standard context accuracy by 10 to 21 points; the pig paper’s own acoustic features score below chance once a lab is held out. A second, smaller result: an affect direction learned on dogs or pigs predicts cat contexts, survives every adversarial test, and is not what it first looked like. All of it came off a laptop in a day.

Three findings, ranked by how much I believe them

Real · the actual finding

Identity dominates

These features identify which of 21 cats is meowing at 0.70–0.79 against a 4.8% chance rate. Any evaluation that lets a cat appear in both train and test is measuring voice recognition. The inflation is +0.10 to +0.20, replicated in all five feature sets.

The room does the work

Delete the meow, keep only the quiet frames between calls: 0.725 on the binary cat task, matching the full embedding. CatMeows induced isolation by moving the cat to a different room, so the label is partly a room label.

Real, modest

Affect transfers dog → cat

A probe trained only on dog barks predicts cat contexts at 0.583, animal-level p = 0.0045, and survives balanced classes (0.618) and strict scaling (0.591). But duration alone gives 0.557, and removing it leaves 0.552. Half the effect is how long the animal calls for.

What a reviewer changed after these results were in

  • The headline was wrong. I led with cross-species transfer. The leakage result is the one that is replicated, mechanistically coherent, and needs no new data. It leads now.
  • “Embeddings transfer where acoustics cannot” was overstated. Log-duration is same-sign in both species (+0.28 / +0.31 SD). The four-feature probe failed because, trained on cats, it loads on energy — which points the other way in dogs and drags it below chance. That is a weighting artifact, not a fact about handcrafted features.
  • The statistics were anti-conservative. Permuting 348 correlated meows as if independent, when identity is 79% decodable, is the wrong exchangeable unit. Re-running with 2,000 animal-level permutations. Also: with 300 permutations the smallest reportable p is 0.0033 — WavLM hit that floor, so it should read “p < 0.0033”.
  • The asymmetry may not exist. 0.583 versus 0.527 with null SDs ~0.04 gives z≈0.95, p≈0.34. All three cat→dog estimates are above chance. The honest phrasing is “underpowered”, never “fails”.

Chance is 0.333 for context, 0.048 for identity. Do not read the ordering as “speech beats bioacoustics” — HuBERT’s 0.571 over AVES’s 0.540 is about 14 clips in a 21-cluster design where per-cat accuracy spans 0.00 to 0.86. The defensible version is the negative: bioacoustic pretraining confers no measurable advantage over speech SSL on domestic-animal vocalisations. Note too that every encoder got best-of-13-layers selection while MFCC got one configuration, and the honest paralinguistics baseline is eGeMAPS (F0, jitter, shimmer, HNR, formants), not MFCC-85. Now run: eGeMAPS scores 0.486 under leave-one-cat-out — so the encoders’ margin over handcrafted features is +0.07 to +0.09, not +0.17. Smaller, and it holds. eGeMAPS shows the same identity leak (0.727) and split inflation (+0.155).

The control that changed the story

Delete the meow. The label survives.

CatMeows induced isolation by carrying the cat into an unfamiliar room. So the negative class differs by recording environment, by construction. I threw away the vocalisation and kept only the quietest 30% of frames.

This is a caveat and a finding

  • Published accuracies on CatMeows are considerably higher than ours. Before believing any of them, check two things: the split, and whether the room is doing the work.
  • The reverb proxy confirms it — energy-decay slope separates the classes at +0.67 / −0.32 SD in dogs and +0.10 / −0.18 in cats.
  • This is the leakage-audit idea from the plan, landing on real data. “Context datasets are partly room datasets” is a paper in itself if it holds across more corpora.

Experiment 3

Train on dogs. Test on cats.

A binary affect axis defensible in both species: negative = cat isolation + dog aggression; positive = cat brushing + dog play. Ambiguous categories (cat waiting-for-food, dog contact) excluded before looking at results. Features z-scored within species, so the probe cannot simply detect which species it is.

Do not explain an asymmetry you have not established

The difference between the two directions is itself not significant (z≈0.95, p≈0.34), and all three cat→dog estimates sit above chance. Explaining a gap before showing it exists is the classic error. If it does survive the asymmetry test, the candidate causes are: Candidate reasons: dogs have the cleaner within-species signal (0.74–0.89 versus 0.69–0.79), so the direction learned from them is better defined; or aggression-vs-play is simply a more acoustically extreme contrast than isolation-vs-brushing; or the inverted class imbalance between the corpora is doing something. Deciding between these is the first experiment of the next session.

Corrected

Three cues invert. One does not.

The standard account — Morton’s motivation-structural rules and the acoustic-universals literature since — says arousal raises pitch, loudness and harshness. I ran four interpretable features through the identical pipeline.

The kill-test verdict — duration is half of it

So the transfer is real at the animal level, but roughly half of what transfers is call duration — the one cue that points the same way in both species. What survives after removing duration is small and needs a third species to mean anything. And the “dogs teach cats but not vice versa” story does not hold up: the two directions are not statistically different. The honest one-liner: an affect axis transfers dog→cat in frozen speech embeddings and survives animal-level, balanced and strict-scaling tests; about half the effect is duration, the residual is small, and the directional asymmetry is not supported. Modest, clean, and exactly the shape of result ICBINB @ NeurIPS asks for. Not the headline. The headline is identity.

Iteration 3

A third species, a wild species, and a hypothesis that died

The reviewer’s first ask was a third corpus from a third lab, to break the two-corpus channel confound and turn a pair into a matrix. Pigs (Soundwel, Briefer et al. 2022, CC-BY-4.0): 5,031 calls after capping, 17 contexts each wholly positive or negative, recorded by six different labs. Bats (Prat et al. 2017 via ESP): 10 wild Egyptian fruit bats, 200 calls each, at 250 kHz.

Hypothesis tested and rejected: “it’s isolation calls”

Pig negatives are dominated by piglet isolation; cat negatives are isolation. If the shared axis were separation distress, removing pig isolation from the source should collapse pig→cat. It moves from 0.591 to 0.568 (still p=0.03); isolation-only as source gives 0.552. Not isolation-specific. Whatever transfers is more general — and still small.

What that means for the transfer claim

It is real, small, and not a stable code. The honest sentence: across three frozen encoders, an affect axis learned on one species predicts cat contexts above chance in every encoder, but the source species that transfers is encoder-dependent and no cell is unanimous; roughly half is call duration; read it as “encoders share some coarse arousal structure across mammals,” not as animals sharing a signal. That is a publishable cautionary result, and it is the opposite of a headline.

Where the two papers stand

  • Paper A, leakage — now three species strong. Cats: individual, +0.10–0.20. Pigs: lab, +0.21, published features below chance across labs. Bats: identity 0.63–0.70 in a wild species. Plus the bandwidth aside. A real, multi-dataset methods paper.
  • Paper B, transfer — smaller and stranger. Real in every encoder, but encoder-dependent in which cell; pig→cat 2 of 3, dog→cat 1 of 3 plus a borderline; placebo-clean, survives duration partialling (borderline), balanced classes, strict scaling, group-level permutation, construct validity; isolation hypothesis rejected. But only into cats, and only one encoder family tested at scale. Honest framing: a cat affect axis is predictable from probes trained on two other species; the reverse is not; the shared component is small, partly duration, and not isolation-specific.

Adversarial

What could still kill this

Written down now, before anyone gets attached to the result.

Two of the four dangers are now eliminated

  • Inverted class imbalance — cleared. Subsampling both species to equal class sizes (127/class cats, 99/class dogs, 200 resamples) gives dog→cat 0.618, 95% range [0.581, 0.648], p < 0.0001. The imbalance was working against the effect, not creating it.
  • Transductive scaling — cleared. Under strict leave-one-target-animal-out scaling, where each held-out cat contributes nothing to its own normalisation, dog→cat is 0.591, p = 0.005. The test-set statistics were not doing the work.
  • Still open, and lethal if it lands: call duration, and the two-corpus channel difference.

Danger 1 — two corpora, two labs

Cats: Italy, 2021. Dogs: Hungary, 2008. Different mics, different decades. Within-species z-scoring removes the mean offset but not a class-correlated channel difference that happens to align across corpora. Settles it: a third species from a fourth lab, or convolving both corpora with a shared set of impulse responses and re-running.

Danger 2 — one contrast each

The “affect axis” is really isolation-vs-brushing mapped onto aggression-vs-play. Those two contrasts might share something specific rather than something general. Settles it: species with several labelled contexts each — bats, pigs, zebra finches.

Danger 3 — inverted imbalance

Cats are negative-heavy (221/127); dogs are negative-light (99/209). The imbalance is inverted between species, which is exactly the configuration where a badly calibrated probe can look like it transfers. Settles it: subsample to equal class sizes, rerun, report both.

Danger 4 — small n, and transductive scaling

Ten dogs, twenty cats; bootstrap CIs are wide. And the target z-score uses test statistics — standard in domain adaptation, but a strict held-out-animal version should be reported. Settles it: more individuals; mixed-effects model with animal as a random effect.

Sequence

What happens next

  1. A third species the decisive one

    Egyptian fruit bats (Prat 2016: ~15k calls with context and emitter ID) or pigs (Briefer 2022: 7,414 calls across 19 valence-labelled contexts). A third corpus from a third lab breaks the two-corpus channel confound and turns two points into a matrix. If dog→bat and dog→pig also work, the finding is about mammals; if only dog→cat works, it is about those two datasets.

    CPU, one evening

  2. Kill the imbalance and the scaling one hour

    Rerun with equal class sizes and with strict held-out-animal z-scoring. Cheap, and it removes two of the four dangers above outright.

    CPU, minutes

  3. Explain the asymmetry

    Three candidate causes, each with a cheap discriminating test: match the within-species accuracy by subsampling; swap which contrast is used; equalise n. Whichever survives explains why dogs teach cats but cats do not teach dogs.

  4. Open the black box connects to chapter 5

    If the direction is not loudness or pitch, what is it? Train a sparse autoencoder on the layer-9 embeddings and look for a feature that fires on distress in both species. This is exactly the SAE chapter from the plan, arriving with a concrete question instead of a vague one.

    novelty-checked: unoccupied

  5. The room paper

    Independently: run the background-only control across every context dataset that is public. If “context datasets are partly room datasets” holds broadly, that is a short, useful, slightly uncomfortable methods paper, and it is the leakage audit the plan already wanted.
